A CMO, for a season

Some companies don't need a whole marketing department yet. They need one senior person who can read the room, make the calls, and leave the team stronger than she found it. Here's how a season with me works.

Who it's for

  • Founders whose growth has stalled or gotten expensive. The product works, customers like it, and the next stage needs someone who has scaled a team before.
  • Companies opening a new market. Especially across borders, where what customers believe about you is still blank.
  • Teams between marketing leaders, or teams that have never had one and are ready to.

If what you need is one campaign or a full-time hire, I'll say so on the first call and point you somewhere better.

What just Claire looks like

I join your leadership team and lead the people you already have. A typical rhythm, over one quarter:

Every week

I run the marketing meeting, coach your leads one-on-one, and clear what's blocking them.

Every month

The numbers on one page your board can read: what we learned, and what we're changing.

Every quarter

The plan, reset with you and the leadership team, so the bets keep up with what customers are telling us.

Interim works the same way, with more days each week, a set end date, and a clean handover to the leader who comes next.

How a season runs

Every company is different, but most seasons move through four phases. Each circle fills as the work moves from me to your team.

  2. Weeks 1 to 3

    Assess

    Customer conversations, the data, the budget, and the team. What do your customers believe, and where do they get stuck?

  3. Weeks 4 to 6

    Strategize

    A few bets, not twenty. What we'll stop doing as well as start, with measurement your finance lead trusts.

  4. Weeks 7 to 12

    Build

    Run the tests, fix the funnel, and hire or coach the people who'll carry the work.

  5. As the season ends

    Transfer

    Your team owns the plan and the numbers. If you're ready for a full-time CMO, I help you find them.

What you have when the season ends

  • A growth plan your team owns and can explain without me in the room.
  • Numbers you trust, and a habit of testing before spending.
  • A team that runs without you, which is the part I'm proudest of from MPOWER: one part-time MBA student grew into a team of 10.

Questions people ask

How much of my time do you need, and how much of yours do I get?

We set it together after the first call, based on what the work needs. A part-time season is a steady rhythm of days each month inside your team. An interim season is closer to full focus for a defined stretch. Either way, you'll know the hours before we start.

Do you work remotely?

Yes. I led a team across the US and India and worked with partners from South Asia to Latin America, so time zones don't scare me. For a kickoff or a big moment, I'm happy to be in the room.

Am I hiring you, or Rarecraft?

Both, in a way. I'm your CMO, and the engagement runs through Rarecraft, the firm I co-founded. So when the plan needs hands as well as a head, for design, paid media, martech, or content, the team is already there.

What industries do you know?

Most of my decade was in fintech and international education at MPOWER Financing, where customers were making one of the biggest decisions of their lives with a brand they'd never heard of. Before that, restaurants, tourism, and small businesses. The work travels well to any company where trust is the hardest part of the sale.

We already have a marketing team. Does this still make sense?

Often it makes the most sense. I work through your team: setting direction, coaching the leads, and clearing the way so their best ideas get a fair hearing.

What does it cost?

It depends on the shape and the hours, so you'll get a clear number in writing after our first call.

Is there a smaller first step?

Yes. Rarecraft runs a free growth audit, which is a good way to see how I think before you commit to a season.

How do we start?

Book a 25-minute call, or send an owl if you'd rather write it down. Tell me where growth is stuck, and I'll come with questions.
