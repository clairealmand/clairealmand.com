Breaking the paid-ads habit

Customer acquisition cost fell 90% while the business kept growing.

In short

The problem
MPOWER leaned on paid ads to reach international students, who trusted friends and fellow students far more than an ad from a company they had never heard of.
What I did
I led a channel-by-channel shift of budget into organic search, a rebuilt referral program, and international student ambassadors, and tightened partner and vendor terms.
The result
Customer acquisition cost fell 90%. In 2023, revenue grew 1.8× on a flat budget.

The problem

Paid ads are a comfortable habit. Money goes in, leads come out, and the dashboard always looks busy.

For a company serving international students, that habit was also an expensive way to meet people. Our future customers trusted their friends, their seniors from back home, and the students who had gone abroad before them. An ad from a brand they had never heard of came a distant last.

The spell

We moved money toward the places students were already listening. Not all at once: each channel earned a bigger share of the budget only after it proved it could carry the weight.

  • Organic search, so the questions students typed at 2 a.m. led to our answers.
  • Referrals, rebuilt so both people got rewarded and the reward arrived sooner.
  • International ambassadors: students who could say "I did this" in the language and context of home.
  • Tighter economics everywhere else: scholarship terms, referral and ambassador terms, and agency and vendor contracts.

The hardest part was emotional, not technical. Turning down a channel that still produces leads feels like turning down growth. The data said otherwise, so we kept listening to the data.

What happened

Customer acquisition cost fell 90%. In 2023, on a flat budget, revenue grew 1.8× and qualified leads grew 2.3×. The controls stuck: customer acquisition cost stayed near historic lows into 2025.

Paid ads stayed in the mix through all of this. In 2025 we cut paid media off entirely, ran an incrementality test, and then brought it back to manage in-house. That came after these results and is a separate story.

Try this at home

Pull your last hundred customers and ask how they first heard of you. If "a friend" shows up far more often than your budget suggests, your money is in the wrong place.

Built with MPOWER's digital team, channel owners, agency partners, and finance. I led the strategy and owned the budget.
